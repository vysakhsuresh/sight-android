Ikshana — the launcher icon, four ways

Each in Android's squircle at 160 and 80 px, and as the themed (monochrome) icon. Ink background, Iris blue #6FD3FF. Everything sits inside the 66 dp safe zone.

A · One line, one eye ★

An open eye drawn as a single unbroken stroke: the upper lid sweeps in, rounds the iris and leaves as the lower lid. The pupil is the one blue dot. Nothing else.

The quietest. Reads at 80 px; the risk is every eye-care and privacy app on the shelf also draws an eye.

B · The eye in the viewfinder

Four camera brackets around the blue pupil, with the lids left out. The brackets say camera, the dot says seeing, and the gap between them is the frame the person holds the phone up to.

Says "camera" first. Brackets are Still's family mark too, so the two apps would rhyme on a shelf.

C · The eye that speaks ★

The same one-line eye, but the lower lid does not close: it runs out to the right as a short wave, the way Stave's staff becomes a sound. Seeing turning into speaking is the whole app in one gesture.

The story of the app in one mark. The wave must stay short or it reads as a tear; holds in one colour.

D · The braille eye

A bold blue iris ring, and inside it the pupil is a braille cell: dots 2 and 4, the letter I, for Ikshana. Sighted people see an eye; a braille reader who has it described hears their own alphabet in it.

The most meaningful and the busiest. At 48 px the cell becomes two specks; the big icon carries it, the small one only the eye.
Masks differ by launcher (circle, squircle, rounded square); every mark sits inside the 66 dp safe zone so none of them clips. The 6.5 dp stroke is what made Stave's final mark readable where its first one smudged.
